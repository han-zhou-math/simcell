# Figure 4 — Actin distributions and non-additive migration

A–B: F- and G-actin fields at 700 s for endpoint pump-strength and pump-width cases. C–D: unsmoothed 5 s F/G concentration-polarity histories. For each species, P=(front mean−rear mean)/(front mean+rear mean); means use the existing equal-weight interior scalar samples split at the area centroid. This study-defined index measures concentration asymmetry, not filament orientation, and is not the front/back concentration ratio plotted in Yao and Li (2022). E: baseline, actin-driven, water-driven (active-solute-pump enabled), and combined cell displacement, with the additive prediction for the central profile (kf=0.12 µm/s, hf=0.105π). Displacement is the laboratory-frame area-centroid shift. F: excess displacement Δd, $d_{AW}-(d_A+d_W-d_0)$ across strength (solid) and width (dashed). The shared central case is drawn once as a thicker red solid line. All 128² cases use the same 100 × 100 µm box, 5 s step, initial F/G state, passive parameters, and 3× water mobilities. The front/rear strength ratio is 3:1 and width ratio is 1:3. Width sweeps also change integrated prescribed pumping capacity. Mean speeds over 100–700 s exceed additive predictions by 7.2–11.4%; the central excess is 10.8% at 128² and 11.2% at 256². These values and the mesh check are retained in supporting tables; former panel H is omitted. The actin switch changes polymerization and active actin forces together; passive actin and passive osmosis remain in every arm; the residual describes non-additivity for that operational switch and is a study-defined diagnostic. The terms actin-driven and water-driven follow Yao and Li (2022), https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.903234. Pump strength denotes a coefficient in µm/s, not the concentration-dependent active solute flux. Pump width is a material-coordinate Gaussian e-folding width.
