# Figure 3 — Water-driven migration

Labels: Equal pumps means equal front/rear peak strengths and widths, not equal fluxes. Wider rear means hr/hf=3 and kr/kf=1/3. Stronger pump keeps that profile and increases both strengths. Subscripts f and r denote front and rear; the front imports solute and the rear exports it. The front points right. F–H inherit the pump settings of C–E and show solute concentration and cell-relative water flow at 2000 s.

A: Laboratory-centroid displacement x_c(t)-x_c(0), including moving-window shifts, over common 0–2000 s. Five symmetric-pump cases (kf in µm/s, hf/π): (0.06,0.105), (0.13,0.070), (0.13,0.105), (0.15,0.105), (0.13,0.210). kr=kf and hr=hf throughout; all use baseline permeability. Strength comparisons hold width at 0.105π; width comparisons hold strength at 0.13 µm/s. These are actual 50 s histories without smoothing, selected for clearer separation. Changing width at fixed peak changes integrated prescribed capacity as well as localization. All curves necessarily meet at zero initially. C–E illustrate separate symmetric/wider-rear choices and do not enumerate A’s cases. The former speed sweep remains in the supporting response table.
B: Three profiles at fixed front strength 0.09 µm/s, all through 2000 s. Relative total interior+exterior solute inventory at50s outputs using polygon/cell fractions and separate one-sided affine reconstruction; a reconstructed diagnostic, not an exact conservation identity.
C–E: Centered shape histories at0,1000,2000s for symmetric0.09, selected asymmetric0.09, asymmetric0.15. Each panel shows one parameter case; scales preserve area differences. Final area ratios: 1.268, 1.254, 2.004.
F–H: Same cases at2000s; solute deviation and cell-relative water streamlines inside/outside. One shared color scale. Streamline density is not speed or membrane flux.
Fixed128²,dt5s,total2000s,fields50s; zero polymerization and initialF-actin,DS100um²/s,kc1um/s,C0=340mM; remaining settings retained. Rear-profile comparison matches initial integrated prescribed coefficient capacity, not actual concentration-dependent solute flux. The strong-pump case reaches A/A0=2.004 (about 100% area increase), so its larger shape reflects substantial swelling as well as deformation. At kf=0.09, the selected asymmetric and symmetric profiles have similar speeds (3.87 versus 3.75 um/h) and area increases (25.4% versus 26.8%), while the asymmetric profile reduces rear indentation. Matched strength times width is prescribed capacity matching, not equality of realized flux. No numerical-significance claim is made for the small speed difference.
